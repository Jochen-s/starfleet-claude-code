# Claim-Evidence Mapping Template

## Purpose
Trace every non-trivial claim to its supporting evidence. This prevents fabrication and makes the research output auditable.

## Template

| # | Claim | Evidence Grade | Sources | Verification Status | Conditions | Caveats |
|---|-------|---------------|---------|-------------------|------------|---------|
| 1 | [Exact claim text] | T1/T2/T3/T4 | [citation keys] | [VERIFIED/ESTIMATED/UNVERIFIED] | [When this claim holds] | [Limitations or qualifications] |

## Evidence Grades

- **T1 (Strong)**: Multiple peer-reviewed sources; reproducible methodology; consistent findings
- **T2 (Moderate)**: 1-2 peer-reviewed sources, or strong preprint/grey literature with quantitative data
- **T3 (Weak)**: Single source, preprint only, or web-source with limited supporting data
- **T4 (Unverified)**: No external source; based on reasoning, extrapolation, or common knowledge

## Verification Status

- **[VERIFIED]**: Claim checked against source; numbers/facts confirmed accurate
- **[ESTIMATED]**: Source provides a range or approximation; exact figure derived
- **[UNVERIFIED]**: Claim not independently verified; flagged for review

## Usage Rules

1. Every quantitative claim (numbers, percentages, comparisons) MUST have an entry
2. Qualitative claims that are non-obvious SHOULD have an entry
3. Common knowledge (e.g., "the sky is blue") does NOT need an entry
4. If a claim has no source, it MUST be graded T4 and marked [UNVERIFIED]
5. Contradicting claims from different sources should both be listed with a note
